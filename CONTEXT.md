# Context

## Glossary

### Creative performer

A person who performs live or semi-live for an audience and needs to react quickly to what people say. This includes emerging comedians, podcast hosts, streamers, improv performers, and event hosts.

### Apprentice comedian

The initial user role: a creative performer who is actively training comedic speed, audience work, callbacks, and improvisational response before performing in front of a real audience.

### Simulated audience member

A fictional audience participant generated by the training simulator. They provide voice-first prompts, personal details, interruptions, and follow-up answers that create material for the apprentice comedian to work with.

### Punchline Arena

The product name. Punchline Arena is a voice-first stage practice room where apprentice comedians train fast crowdwork, callbacks, contradictions, and comedic connections with simulated audiences.

### Training simulator

The initial product shape: a practice environment where a creative performer trains comedic speed against simulated audience interactions. The simulator helps the performer develop their own instincts rather than feeding lines during a real performance.

### Stage practice room

The primary training environment: an illustrated performance space with simulated audience members and voice-first interaction. It should feel like rehearsing in a comedy venue rather than using a generic chat or analytics dashboard.

### Voice-first practice

The apprentice comedian responds primarily through a microphone. Text can exist as a fallback or transcript, but the training value comes from practicing spoken timing, brevity, and reaction under pressure.

### Connection graph

A visual map of comedic links formed during a training session. It shows people, details, premises, contradictions, callbacks, and repeated themes so the apprentice comedian can see how the show material connects.

The connection graph is the product's central demo moment: it should appear to grow as the session unfolds, proving that the simulator is tracking context rather than producing isolated joke suggestions.

### Riff

A short, performer-owned response built from the current conversation, prior context, and the performer's own taste. The product may suggest directions for a riff, but the performer remains the author of what is said.

### Callback

A comedic connection that refers back to a memorable detail mentioned earlier in the same session.

### Contradiction

A comedic opportunity created when a person's statements, identity, goals, or choices clash in a surprising or socially recognizable way.

### Skill lane

A category of comedic thinking that the training simulator can isolate and practice, such as callbacks, contradictions, escalation, character angles, soft roasts, follow-up questions, premises, misdirection, tags, and rule-of-three patterns.

### Live cue

A short signal shown during a practice round to draw the apprentice comedian's attention to an opportunity without giving them a full line. Examples include "callback available", "contradiction", "strong question", and "escalate".

### Post-round analysis

The detailed feedback shown after a practice round. It explains detected connections, missed opportunities, alternative routes, and training scores without interrupting the performer's timing during the round.

### Riff route

A structured direction for building a riff from an opportunity. A riff route explains the comedic angle, why it works, and possible framing without presenting a finished joke as the required answer.

### Comedy mentor profile

A general training lens that describes patterns used by fast comedians, such as callbacks, audience labeling, contrast, compression, escalation, and timing. It must not imitate or clone a specific living comedian's protected style or rely on copyrighted transcripts without permission.

### Fast Crowdwork Mentor

The initial comedy mentor profile. It teaches seven general techniques: detail catching, audience labeling, contradiction mining, callback setup, escalation ladder, follow-up forks, and compression and tagging.

### Detail catching

The technique of noticing specific details in an audience member's answer that could later become material.

### Audience labeling

The technique of turning an audience member's details into a playful, memorable character label.

### Contradiction mining

The technique of finding clashes between what a person says they are, what they want, and what they do.

### Callback setup

The technique of saving a detail as a future callback seed.

### Escalation ladder

The technique of pushing an idea through levels from realistic to exaggerated, absurd, and surreal.

### Follow-up fork

The technique of asking a question that is funny by itself and also opens more material.

### Compression and tagging

The technique of reducing a comedic observation into a short spoken line and adding small follow-up hits.

### Private reference corpus

Local-only reference material used to study public comedy performances and extract general technique patterns. It is not part of the product, repository, demo assets, or submission, and it must not be used to reproduce protected jokes, transcripts, likenesses, or a specific comedian's style.

### Training score

A skill-specific evaluation of a spoken riff. The simulator avoids generic "funny scores" and instead evaluates trainable dimensions such as memory, specificity, tension, brevity, follow-up potential, and performer ownership.

### Practice round

A 60-90 second training loop in the stage practice room. The apprentice comedian chooses a room, receives audience prompts, responds by microphone, sees short live cues, and then reviews a post-round analysis with transcripts, connections, missed opportunities, alternative routes, and skill-specific scores.

### Open Mic Bar

The first playable stage practice room. It is a small comedy venue with a simulated audience, voice-first prompts, a timer, live cues, and a connection graph.

### Video learner

The primary user for the generic Obsidian sync route: a person who learns from videos and wants to turn selected time ranges into connected personal knowledge, not just summaries.

### Atomic video note

A small, self-contained knowledge note extracted from a specific video time range. It contains one idea, source metadata, timestamp evidence, and suggested links to other notes or canonical knowledge areas.

### Vault map

A lightweight representation of an Obsidian vault's existing folders, note titles, tags, and canonical areas. The product uses it to suggest where new atomic video notes should belong and what they should connect to.
